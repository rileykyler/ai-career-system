## How I got into energy

The energy markets course started it. Electricity cannot be stored at scale, has to be balanced against demand every few minutes, and gets priced by location, and ERCOT runs that with no capacity market, an isolated grid, and the fastest load growth in the country.

Rather than let that sit as a class I enjoyed, I went and found people to talk to. I met in person with a senior inventory manager at Jupiter and with Tom Lacy at LG. Between the two conversations I got very different angles on the same industry: how storage assets actually get built and operated, where analytics sit in the decision-making, and what the commercial side of BESS looks like in practice.

That gave me enough direction to study seriously on my own. ERCOT market structure, day-ahead and real-time, SCED, LMP and congestion, ancillary services, the QSE role, settlements, and battery dispatch economics, including how merchant, contracted, and hybrid BESS strategies differ in where they actually earn.

From there I was sponsored to attend Datacloud USA by the Young Talent in Digital Infrastructure cohort. I spent it with people currently practicing, and the through-line of nearly every conversation was the supply and demand gap: data center load growth, time to power, interconnection queues, transformer and switchgear lead times, and where storage fits against all of it. That event did more for my understanding than anything I had read, because it was practitioners describing constraints they are living with right now.
