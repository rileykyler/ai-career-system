# Fisher Investments — AE Round 2 Study Guide (Zoom today)

Read this once, out loud where you can, then go. It's a personality-driven conversation, not a knowledge test.

## The two answers to actually nail

**"How do you handle rejection?"**
"When I get a no, I treat it as information about my approach, not a reflection on me — at Crunch I track my own conversion numbers specifically so I can see where I'm losing people and adjust, rather than just pushing harder the same way. I'd bring that same habit here."

**"What do you do outside of work / are you part of any groups?"**
TTHA at Texas State — your roommate was president, the org regularly hosted events, you were part of pulling them off. Have ONE specific event in mind before the call. Don't just say "I was a member."

## If asked about the five traits (know the substance, say it like a story not a script)

- **Drive Results →** 19% qualified-lead conversion, ~12% above target on average, 17% higher client lifetime value, 83% retention, 96% satisfaction at Crunch. You used the numbers proactively, not just reviewed them after.
- **Instill Trust →** Pricing objection story: asked questions to find the real blocker instead of discounting/arguing, pointed the person to a better-fit option when that was honestly right. Ties naturally to Fisher's fee-based/fiduciary model if it comes up.
- **Communication →** Lowe's: tracked service issues and coordinated between customers and internal teams — different audience than Crunch, shows range.
- **Persuade →** Your conversion numbers are the evidence — getting 19% of qualified leads to commit is persuasion with a track record. Pricing-objection story works here too.
- **Committed →** Trainer Workshop — nobody asked you to fix that trainers were solving the same problems alone, you built a recurring workshop yourself, it got adopted franchise-wide.

## Company basics, if asked "what do you know about Fisher"
Independent, privately-held, fee-based fiduciary money manager, founded by Ken Fisher, serves high-net-worth individuals and institutions. This AE role works warm, marketing-generated leads — people who already engaged with Fisher's own materials — not cold prospecting. Say this last part explicitly; it shows you understood the actual role.

## Questions to ask them
- What does a typical week look like in terms of call volume and lead flow?
- How is success measured in the first 90 days?
- What does the path from AE to an outside Investment Counselor role actually look like?
- What separates AEs who do well here from those who don't?

## Mindset for the call
Know all of the above cold, but say it conversationally — like you're telling a friend what happened, not reciting a memorized answer. That's the actual note from someone who just did this exact interview and got the job.
