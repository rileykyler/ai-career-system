# Resume Tailoring System — Mandatory Workflow

**2026-09-10 — supersedes the old lightweight 9-section template.** This is
now the required process for every posting before a resume gets built, not
just a form to fill in after the fact. It replaces the previous
Job-Description-Analysis-Template.md outline; Master-Profile.md Section 13 and
the "Daily Applications" scheduled task both point here.

Every resume must be built specifically for the individual company AND
individual job posting.

Do not simply select bullets from a static bullet bank (Resume-Profile-Sales.md
/ Resume-Profile-Energy.md's Crunch Fitness bullet banks are the *source
material*, not a fixed final answer).

Existing verified experience, metrics, tools, coursework, and accomplishments
(Master-Profile.md) are the factual source of truth, but the wording,
ordering, emphasis, subsection names, competencies, and bullet construction
should be rebuilt for each role when doing so improves relevance.

Never invent experience or turn adjacent experience into direct experience.

---

## PHASE 1 — Company Research

Before tailoring the resume, research the company. Use current public sources
when available, prioritizing: official company website, careers page, the job
posting itself, about/mission/values pages, product or services pages, recent
company news, investor materials if public, LinkedIn/company materials when
useful.

Determine:

**Company Business** — What does the company sell or provide? Who are its
customers? B2B, B2C, enterprise, SMB, government, industrial? How does it make
money? What markets does it operate in?

**Company Priorities** — growth priorities, products/services emphasized,
industries served, customer types, geographic focus, technology strategy,
operational priorities, current expansion or business changes.

**Company Language** — extract recurring language the company itself uses
(e.g. customer success, operational excellence, pipeline management, market
intelligence, commercial execution, ownership, urgency, analytical rigor,
cross-functional collaboration, revenue growth). Do NOT copy meaningless
corporate language just because it appears on the website — prioritize
language that connects directly to the role.

**Company Culture Signals** — repeated behavioral themes (ownership,
curiosity, speed, accountability, collaboration, customer obsession,
innovation, entrepreneurial mindset, analytical thinking). These should
influence positioning only when Riley's verified experience actually supports
them.

## PHASE 2 — Job Description Decomposition

Analyze the posting before touching the resume. Separate into:

**A. Core Function** — "What is this person actually being hired to
accomplish?" One to three sentences. Do not just repeat the job title.

**B. Primary Responsibilities** — rank mission-critical / important /
secondary. Estimate what the employee will likely spend most of their time
doing.

**C. Required Qualifications** — extract every true requirement (degree,
years of experience, software, industry knowledge, technical knowledge,
certifications, sales experience, analytical skills, communication skills).

**D. Preferred Qualifications** — kept strictly separate from required. Never
treat a preferred qualification as mandatory.

## PHASE 3 — ATS / Keyword Map

Build a structured keyword map from the posting — not a giant flat list.
Classify into:

- **Tier 1 — Critical Role Keywords** (e.g. sales operations, supply chain, account management, market analysis, customer intelligence, pipeline management)
- **Tier 2 — Tools / Systems** (Excel, Salesforce, Power BI, SAP, CRM)
- **Tier 3 — Industry / Domain Terms** (ERCOT, logistics, SaaS, BESS, data centers, energy markets)
- **Tier 4 — Responsibility Language** (analyze, coordinate, monitor, forecast, track, identify, resolve, optimize, report, support)
- **Tier 5 — Behavioral Signals** (ownership, communication, attention to detail, urgency, collaboration)

For each important keyword, mark **VERIFIED DIRECT** (Riley can truthfully use
this exact concept), **TRANSFERABLE** (related experience exists, but don't
imply direct experience), or **GAP** (Riley doesn't have it — check
Skills-Gap-Tracker.md for known status first rather than guessing fresh).
Never insert a GAP keyword as a "skill" just to raise ATS match rate.

## PHASE 4 — Candidate Gap Map

Compare the role against Master-Profile.md (and Skills-Gap-Tracker.md for
tool/skill status specifically). For each major requirement, classify
**DIRECT** (verified experience directly demonstrates it), **ADJACENT**
(relevant transferable experience — explain the connection), or **GAP** (no
meaningful evidence). This is the same DIRECT/ADJACENT/GAP standard
Master-Profile.md Section 13 already requires and that Job-Fit-Scoring-Rubric.md's
Skills Match criterion scores against — this phase is where that
classification actually gets done, in writing, before scoring or building.

Example — JOB: "Pipeline management." RILEY: has tracked qualified leads,
referrals, follow-up, re-engagement, renewals, customer expansion.
Classification: ADJACENT / strong transferable. Truthful wording: "Tracked
qualified opportunities and follow-up activity across leads, referrals,
re-engagement, renewals, and account expansion." Do NOT write "Managed
enterprise B2B sales pipeline" — that upgrades ADJACENT to DIRECT, which
Master-Profile.md explicitly forbids.

## PHASE 5 — Resume Positioning Strategy

Before writing bullets, decide the story: "If the recruiter remembers only
three things about Riley after reading this resume, what should those three
things be?"

Example (supply chain): 1) data-driven operational thinker, 2) strong
tracking/coordination/issue-resolution ability, 3) proven process improvement
and ownership.
Example (SDR): 1) proven measurable sales performance, 2) consultative
customer communication, 3) pipeline follow-up and disciplined KPI tracking.
Example (energy analyst): 1) analytical business background, 2) Excel/
performance-analysis experience, 3) intentional energy-market education and
interest.

Everything on the page should support the selected positioning.

**Cross-check Interview-Story-Library.md** once positioning is chosen — if one
of its STAR stories reinforces the same three things (it usually will, since
they're drawn from the same verified experience), note which one. This
doesn't change the resume itself, but it keeps the story library connected to
the actual application instead of being a standalone reference nobody opens —
surface the match if Riley asks for a cover letter or interview prep for this
posting.

## PHASE 6 — Rebuild Each Section

Don't just change Core Competencies — tailor every appropriate section.

**Core Competencies** — choose terminology based on role requirements, ATS
language (Phase 3), and Riley's verified experience. Use the company's
vocabulary (Phase 1) when it accurately describes Riley's background.

**Relevant Coursework** — prioritize only relevant *verified* coursework from
Master-Profile.md's 7 approved courses (never invent a course). Supply chain
role -> Supply Chain Management, Operations Management, Business Analytics,
Project Management. Energy role -> Energy Markets & Renewable Energy, Business
Analytics, Operations Management. Sales role -> prioritize business/analytics/
strategy material appropriately.

**Industry Interests** — change emphasis per opportunity. Don't force energy
language onto an unrelated supply-chain role, or vice versa.

**Professional Experience** — the critical tailoring area (see Phases 7-9).

## PHASE 7 — Dynamic Experience Subsections

Subsection headings under Crunch Fitness (and Lowe's, when useful) should be
tailored per posting — they are positioning tools, not fixed labels. Examples:

- Sales role: Sales Performance & Conversion / Pipeline & Customer Growth / Account Management & Retention
- Operations role: Operational Performance / Process Improvement / Customer & Workflow Management
- Supply chain role: Operations & Process Coordination / Performance Tracking / Customer & Issue Resolution
- Customer intelligence role: Customer Performance Analytics / Retention & Growth / Customer Engagement

Only use a heading that accurately describes the bullets underneath it.

## PHASE 8 — Bullet Rewriting Rule

Every bullet should ideally communicate ACTION + WHAT WAS MANAGED/ANALYZED +
BUSINESS PURPOSE/RESULT. Prioritize measurable results, scale, process,
decisions, business impact. Avoid generic filler ("Responsible for helping
customers"). Prefer something like: "Managed an active client portfolio, using
engagement, attendance, and package-utilization data to prioritize follow-up
and maintain 83% retention and 96% satisfaction." Don't force every bullet to
contain a number, though.

## PHASE 9 — Language Mirroring

Mirror the employer's language when: the term accurately describes Riley's
experience, the wording sounds natural, it improves recruiter understanding,
and it improves ATS relevance. Example: posting repeatedly says
"cross-functional coordination"; existing experience is "worked with
customers and internal teams" -> rewrite as "Coordinated across customers and
internal teams to track service issues and corrective actions through
resolution." That's legitimate. Do NOT keyword-stuff, repeat phrases
unnaturally, or copy entire sentences from the posting.

## PHASE 10 — ATS Quality Check

Before finalizing, inspect for:

**Parsing** — standard section headings, simple reading order, no essential
information only inside headers/footers, no icons replacing words, no critical
information stored solely in graphical elements, no excessive columns, no
unusual characters.

**Keyword coverage** — a private checklist: `CRITICAL KEYWORD | PRESENT? |
WHERE? | VERIFIED?` (e.g. `Excel | YES | Tools + Experience | DIRECT`,
`Inventory Management | NO | - | GAP`, `SAP | NO | - | GAP`). Never insert an
unsupported keyword just to fill the checklist.

**Resolved 2026-09-10:** the currently approved visual format
(Resume-Formatting-Spec.md, modeled on the Voltus reference) uses simple
single-level tables for the grid sections (Core Competencies, Coursework,
Industry Interests, Tools) and the gray section-header bars, inside an
otherwise single-column page. Riley confirmed keeping this format as-is,
per direct guidance from an Indeed executive on how Indeed's own resume
parsing handles it, and because a single-column page with plain grids (no
merged cells, no nested tables, no text boxes, no icons-for-words) is the
lower-risk end of "using tables" regardless. Don't rebuild a second ATS-safe
version unless Riley asks for one for a specific application channel — e.g. a
company career-site portal that isn't Indeed-mediated, where table handling
is less certain than it is for Indeed itself.

## PHASE 11 — Recruiter 10-Second Test

Pretend you're the recruiter. Read only: name/header, core competencies,
education, first 2 bullets under the current job, previous job title, tools.
Ask: Is the candidate's relevance obvious? Do I understand what they've
accomplished? Are measurable results visible? Does their background plausibly
connect to this role? Is anything confusing or inflated? If not, revise.

## PHASE 12 — Hiring-Manager Test

Go deeper: "What concerns would the hiring manager have about Riley?" List the
top 3. Determine whether truthful resume evidence can reduce those concerns.
If yes, revise. If no, leave the gap honest — don't paper over it.

## PHASE 13 — Final Tailoring Report

Before generating the final resume, internally produce:

- **Target Positioning** — the three things the recruiter should remember
- **Strongest Matches** — top 5
- **Transferable Matches** — top 3
- **Major Gaps** — top 3
- **Critical ATS Terms Used** — list
- **Critical ATS Terms Not Used** — list and explain why
- **Changes Made** — bullets reordered, bullets rewritten, subsection headings
  changed, competencies changed, coursework emphasis changed, industry
  emphasis changed

Then generate the final resume. (This report doesn't have to be shown in the
delivered brief by default, but Riley can ask for it — a condensed version can
go into the brief's per-listing rationale, or the full report can be produced
on request for any specific pick.)

---

## Absolute Rule

The objective is NOT "make Riley appear to satisfy every qualification." The
objective is "present Riley's real background using the language, evidence,
ordering, and emphasis most likely to make the specific employer recognize his
relevance." **Truthfulness overrides ATS optimization.**

## Indeed API call pacing (added 2026-09-14)

`get_job_details` hit a persistent rate limit three runs in a row
(2026-09-11, 2026-09-12, 2026-09-13) — cooldowns that fluctuated between
~15-90 seconds and didn't reliably clear even after multi-minute waits once
triggered. Root cause looks like an account-level quota on the Indeed
connector, not something fixable from inside a run, so the fix is call
discipline, not filtering shortcuts:

1. **Call `get_job_details` one at a time, not in parallel batches.** Firing
   5-8 detail calls simultaneously appears to trip the limit faster than the
   same number spread out. Space them out.
2. **Cap retries at 2-3 attempts per blocked call.** If cooldowns are still
   escalating/fluctuating after that, stop retrying for the rest of the run
   and move whatever's left unverified to the "Needs follow-up" section of
   Jobs-Seen-Log.md (established pattern from the 2026-09-12 and 2026-09-13
   runs) rather than burning 10+ minutes on it or, worse, scoring on
   incomplete preview data.
3. **Verify the highest-confidence candidates first** (strong comp signal,
   recognizable company, clear track fit from the preview), so if the limit
   does hit partway through, the listings most likely to matter are already
   secured before it does.

**Explicitly rejected approach — do not pre-filter from `search_jobs` preview
data alone** (title, company, comp range, location) to avoid detail calls.
Job-Fit-Scoring-Rubric.md's hard filters were deliberately written to require
the actual posting text, not the title — e.g. Filter 8 says outright "title
alone is not sufficient evidence either way — read the requirements," and
Filters 2/6/7 all turn on specifics (licensing terms, degree-substitution
language, which qualifications are actually mandatory) that never show up in
a search-results preview. Guessing from preview data alone would silently
reintroduce the exact over-filtering (false rejects) and under-filtering
(false accepts) problem those rules were written on 2026-09-10 to prevent —
that's a worse outcome than a slower run. The one narrow exception: skipping
a detail call for a listing that's a near-duplicate of a company+title
pattern already confirmed with full text in a prior run (e.g. the same
roofing company reposting under a slightly different title, or the same
SelectQuote req reappearing in a different state) — that's reusing confirmed
evidence, not guessing from a title.

**Connector completely fails to connect (added 2026-09-15):** distinct from
the rate-limit pattern above — this is a protocol/dial error where the whole
MCP server is unreachable and *zero* `search_jobs`/`get_job_details` calls
succeed, immediately, not after some number of good calls. Confirmed on
2026-09-15's scheduled run and confirmed *recovered* with no code change on
the very next (interactive) session the same day — so this looks like a
transient infrastructure hiccup unrelated to being unattended, not something
a pacing rule can fix. If the very first Indeed call of a run fails this way,
don't burn more than one quick retry on it — note it in the run summary and
proceed with Greenhouse (+ Glassdoor per the fetch-approval-gate workaround
below) for the rest of that run, exactly as the 2026-09-15 run did.

## Fetch-approval gate — Glassdoor and the Greenhouse API (added 2026-09-15)

Both `glassdoor.com` and `boards-api.greenhouse.io` sit behind a WebFetch
provenance/approval step that needs a live person to resolve it. It reliably
resolves in an interactive session (confirmed working 2026-09-15) and
reliably times out — `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED` — in a scheduled/unattended run,
on every single attempt including immediate retries of the identical URL
(also confirmed 2026-09-15, same day). This is a session-liveness property,
not a rate limit or a bad URL, so retrying the same call does not help and
just wastes the run's time budget.

**Rule of thumb for a scheduled/unattended run:** try one call to
`boards-api.greenhouse.io` (or a Glassdoor search URL) at the very start of
that source's leg. If it succeeds, proceed normally — nothing else changes.
If it returns `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED` even once, treat the whole endpoint as
blocked for the rest of that run (don't retry board-by-board) and switch
immediately to the fallback:

- **Greenhouse:** use the WebSearch-then-individual-job-page-WebFetch method
  documented in Greenhouse-Target-Boards.md's "Fetch-approval gate" section —
  `job-boards.greenhouse.io/<token>/jobs/<id>` posting pages are not gated
  and work fine even when the API and the board's own index page are blocked.
- **Glassdoor:** don't WebFetch any `glassdoor.com` URL, including a plain
  search-results page — that's gated too. Use `WebSearch` for
  `site:glassdoor.com <role> <location>`-style queries to get title / company
  / location / snippet only from the search results themselves (search
  results are not gated, only the fetched page is), then verify and score
  from the employer's own careers page or a Greenhouse posting — never from a
  fetched Glassdoor page. This is the same fallback the daily-run prompt
  already specifies for Glassdoor detail pages generally ("get its full text
  from the employer's own careers page or Greenhouse board instead"); the
  2026-09-15 finding is just that the *search* page needs the same treatment
  in an unattended run, not only the detail pages.
- **Live/interactive run:** both endpoints can work — try them first for the
  efficiency (one call pulls a whole board's full text via the Greenhouse
  API, versus one WebSearch + one WebFetch per candidate posting), but
  abandon on the first `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED` rather than retrying, and fall
  back to the same workarounds above for the remainder of that run.
